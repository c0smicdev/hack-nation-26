---
name: Describe workflow
trigger: When the expert says which workflow they want to show you, describes what they do, or says when or for whom the task comes up.
---

Build a short brief of the workflow before you watch it, so you can make sense of what you'll later see on screen. Collect only enough to start observing; the steps themselves you'll learn by watching.

1. Listen to what they describe. As soon as you understand anything about the task, call [tool name="update_workflow"] with a title and a description, then keep talking. Call it again every time you learn something new, so the fields on their screen stay current.
   - `title`: 3–7 words, the task as they'd name it ("Process supplier invoices").
   - `description`: 2–5 plain sentences, the brief: what the task achieves and when it comes up, what a finished job looks like, the broad stages, and any risky decisions or situations where the normal process changes, as far as they told you. Use their words. Refine it; never drop facts they gave, never invent any. It's what they expect to happen, not something you've seen.
2. Ask one short question at a time about what's still missing, most valuable first:
   - what the task achieves and when it comes up (you need both before you can start),
   - decisions that are especially important or risky,
   - situations where the normal process changes,
   - how they know the job is done right,
   - the broad stages.

   Skip anything they already covered. Never ask for click-by-click instructions. Ask at most four questions in total, usually two or three, because every question delays the real work.

3. Continue with [procedure name="Create workflow"] once all of these are true: you know what the task achieves; you know when it comes up, as a concrete trigger in their words ("before the 25th", "when an invoice arrives"), not a guess from a word like "monthly"; and you know where the judgment is (a risky decision, a limit, or a situation where the normal process changes), because that's what you'll watch for. Ask about whatever of these is missing, even right after their first sentence. After four questions, continue regardless. Never ask whether you should set it up or whether they're ready: go straight on, because they can edit everything later and every extra turn delays the real work.
